A little about me

I am an ICI Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Astronomy at the University of Virginia.

My research examines gas- and ice-phase chemistry in star- and planet-forming environments. I combine laboratory experiments, astronomical observations, chemical modeling, and radiative transfer to investigate how irradiation, temperature, and physical structure influence molecular abundances and volatile evolution.

In the laboratory, I study the desorption and infrared properties of astrophysical ice analogues using cryogenic ultra-high-vacuum experiments. My observational work includes JWST spectroscopy of molecular ices in protostellar environments and developing studies of externally irradiated Orion silhouette disks. I also model disk gas and ice chemistry and use synthetic observations to investigate which chemical signatures can actually be recovered from astronomical data.

Laboratory Astrochemistry

I investigate the physical and chemical behavior of astrophysical ice analogues using cryogenic ultra-high-vacuum experiments. My research examines how ice composition and structure affect molecular desorption and infrared spectral properties, including experiments on methyl formate and developing studies of CO2 ice porosity.

JWST Observations

I use JWST spectroscopy to investigate molecular ices in protostellar and protoplanetary environments. For CORINOS IV, I developed a Monte Carlo baseline-fitting approach to quantify uncertainties in weak, blended SO2 ice absorption features in MIRI spectra. Current work includes developing NIRSpec studies of ice survival in Orion silhouette disks.

Chemical Modeling

I develop astrochemical models to study how external UV irradiation, disk structure, and molecular binding energies affect gas- and ice-phase abundances in protoplanetary disks. This work connects physical environments with chemical tracers, snowline locations, and synthetic molecular-line observations.

Disk chemistry paper Close

Radiative Transfer

I use radiative-transfer calculations to investigate how molecular signatures are shaped by the paths light takes through a disk. By constructing synthetic observations of edge-on and silhouette disks, I examine how scattering, geometry, and spectral extraction affect the recovery of ice absorption and observable environmental signatures. This work is ongoing.
