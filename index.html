About Me

I am an ICI Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Astronomy at the University of Virginia, working with Prof. Ilse Cleeves and Prof. Catherine Dukes. I received my Ph.D. in Chemistry from UVA in May 2026.

My research focuses on astrochemistry in star- and planet-forming environments, with a particular interest in the physical chemistry of astrophysical ices and their relationship to gas-phase abundances. My work spans cryogenic laboratory experiments, JWST infrared spectroscopy, chemical modeling, and radiative transfer, investigating molecular processes from protostellar environments to protoplanetary disks.

Current Research

Ongoing · Radiative transfer

When does disk ice leave an observable signature of external UV processing?

External UV irradiation can alter the ice composition of exposed disk regions, while the denser inner disk remains comparatively shielded, potentially preserving its volatile reservoir. However, determining whether these environmental effects are observable is complicated by radiative-transfer effects: infrared ice absorption depends not only on the underlying molecular abundances, but also on disk geometry, dust scattering, optical depth, and the regions contributing to an extracted spectrum.

I combine disk chemical models with radiative-transfer simulations to generate synthetic images and infrared spectra of edge-on disks under different external UV conditions. By comparing the recovered H2O, CO2, and CO ice signatures with their physical distributions, I investigate whether apparent changes in ice absorption reliably trace UV-driven chemical differences and how effectively observations probe shielded material. I am extending this framework to Orion silhouette disks, where the bright nebular background provides a transmitted light source for probing ice absorption through the disk.

In development · Laboratory astrochemistry

How does CO2 ice structure affect its spectrum?

My current laboratory work is developing experiments to test how CO2 ice porosity influences its infrared spectral properties and the abundances inferred from those spectra, motivated in part by observations of CO2 on Ganymede.

This project builds on my experience developing, operating, and troubleshooting cryogenic ultra-high-vacuum instrumentation. I am also helping re-establish our UHV setup in UVA's new KEVION facility , which combines ion-irradiation capabilities with specialized analytical chambers for experimental space science.

Laboratory experiments · 2026

Does the ice matrix matter for methyl formate desorption?

Gas-phase methyl formate (MF) has been detected in protostellar environments at temperatures below those typically associated with bulk H2O ice sublimation. These observations are difficult to reconcile with models that assume complex organic molecules (COMs) remain bound to water-rich ice mantles until H2O desorbs. To investigate whether ice composition can facilitate desorption at lower temperatures, I conducted temperature-programmed desorption (TPD) experiments to characterize the binding energies and desorption behavior of MF on H2O- and CH3OH-rich substrates across a range of molecular coverages.

Our results demonstrate that methanol-rich ice mantles substantially lower MF binding energies relative to water-rich substrates, enabling thermal desorption at lower temperatures. This establishes ice composition as an important parameter in regulating COM desorption and provides an experimentally supported pathway for MF to enter the gas phase before bulk water-ice sublimation. These measurements offer constraints for improving binding-energy prescriptions in astrochemical models.

JWST spectroscopy · CORINOS IV

How confidently can we measure a weak ice feature?

Constraining SO2 Ice in Protostellar Envelopes with JWST

Despite the substantial depletion of sulfur from the gas phase in dense molecular environments, its dominant solid-state reservoirs remain poorly constrained. SO2 ice is a potential contributor, but its weak absorption near 7.6 μm overlaps the stronger CH4 ice feature, complicating its identification and abundance determination. In CORINOS IV, I analyzed JWST/MIRI spectra of four Class 0 protostars and developed a Monte Carlo baseline-fitting approach to quantify the uncertainties associated with continuum placement and laboratory ice decomposition.

We found evidence for SO2 ice in three of the four sources, with the noisy spectrum of B335 preventing a definitive detection. Our best-fitting H2O: SO2 laboratory mixtures suggest that approximately 0.2–0.9% of volatile sulfur is contained in SO2 ice. While the stronger CH4 band proved relatively insensitive to continuum selection, the inferred SO2 abundance depended on both baseline placement and the assumed ice composition, emphasizing the need to propagate these uncertainties when measuring weak, blended ice features.

Chemical modeling · 2025

What changes when a disk forms near massive stars?

The models reveal a spatially and species-dependent chemical response. External UV irradiation establishes an ionization front in the outer disk, with C+, atomic C, N2H+, and CS exhibiting greater sensitivity to the incident radiation field than CO and HCO+. These effects are more pronounced in extended disks with less shielding. In contrast, the inner ~25 au remains comparatively protected from external irradiation, preserving much of its underlying chemistry even as the outer disk is altered.

These results demonstrate that moderate external UV irradiation can produce distinct chemical signatures without uniformly altering the entire disk. The relative shielding of the inner ~25 au has important implications for planet formation, suggesting that material in these regions may retain a chemical composition comparable to that in disks forming in more quiescent environments, even when their outer disk chemistry differs substantially.
