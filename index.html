A little about me

Rachel Gross

ICI Postdoctoral Fellow · Department of Astronomy, University of Virginia

I am an ICI Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Virginia, working with Prof. Ilse Cleeves and Prof. Catherine Dukes. I received my Ph.D. in Chemistry from UVA in May 2026.

I am particularly interested in astrophysical ices: how their composition and structure change with their surroundings, when molecules desorb into the gas, and which icy materials survive into planet-forming disks. Because gas and ice are chemically connected, I also study gas-phase abundances and molecular tracers.

I bring together cryogenic laboratory experiments, JWST spectroscopy, chemical modeling, and radiative transfer to connect the physical chemistry of gas and ice with what we can actually infer from astronomical observations.

Laboratory Astrochemistry

Published work — methyl formate desorption. Complex organic molecules are often assumed to remain associated with H2O-rich ice until the water matrix sublimates, yet methyl formate is observed in gas at lower temperatures. Using temperature-programmed desorption experiments, we measured its behavior on amorphous and crystalline H2O and CH3OH substrates. We found weaker binding on CH3OH ice, with characteristic desorption near 85 K rather than roughly 95–100 K on H2O substrates. The result shows how the surrounding ice can affect when complex organics enter the gas, without requiring bulk water-ice sublimation.

Current work — CO2 ice. I am developing experiments to examine how CO2 ice porosity affects its infrared spectral properties, motivated in part by the interpretation of CO2 observations and abundance estimates on Ganymede.

JWST Observations

Published work — CORINOS IV. I analyzed JWST/MIRI spectra of four Class 0 protostars to investigate weak SO2 ice absorption near the stronger CH4 band. Because the inferred SO2 abundance depends strongly on continuum placement and the adopted laboratory ice spectrum, I developed a randomized baseline-fitting approach to propagate that uncertainty through the spectral fits. This analysis places bounds on SO2 as a potential reservoir of volatile sulfur in protostellar ices.

Chemical Modeling

Published work — externally irradiated disk chemistry. With chemical models of protoplanetary disks exposed to moderate external UV fields (10–100 G0), I investigated how the surrounding stellar environment changes gas- and ice-phase abundances. The response depends on disk structure: the outer regions and tracers such as N2H+, C, and CS are affected more strongly, while the inner tens of au remain comparatively shielded. I also generated synthetic molecular-line predictions to explore observable signatures of irradiation.

Related modeling. I am investigating how updated molecular binding energies change freeze-out, snowline locations, and the resulting gas and ice chemistry.

Radiative Transfer

Current work — recovering disk ice signatures. I use RADMC-3D to generate synthetic observations of externally irradiated, edge-on protoplanetary disks and compare the ice absorption inferred from spectra with the underlying physical ice distributions. By varying disk size, extraction position, and irradiation, I am testing how scattering and viewing geometry can conceal or alter an environmental signature.

Next step — Orion silhouettes. I am extending these calculations toward silhouette disks, where diffuse nebular light is transmitted through the disk. Comparing this geometry with conventional scattered-light observations will help establish which features of the ice distribution can actually be recovered from JWST spectra.
